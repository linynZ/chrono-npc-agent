\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.} This is a single case. One game, three personas from
one era, one cloud model and three small local ones on one 8\,GB laptop GPU;
none of the numbers should be read as a property of the models in general.
The held-out set has 23 attacks, written by one author; five rounds give
intervals on sampling noise, not on the choice of cases, and a different
author would write different attacks. Every case is a single turn, while real
players iterate. The judge belongs to the cloud model's family; we audited
every flagged reply, but replies the judge passed and the substring check did
not flag were not individually audited, so leak counts are lower bounds.
The audit was performed by the author with AI assistance and has not been
checked by a second rater. Hosted-model behaviour drifts, and our two
measurement dates already disagree on latency. Finally, we measured whether a
reply \emph{could} help a player, not whether players use NPCs this way.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Context-graded leakage---sharing a fact in
conversation, refusing it when the player is holding the question---is the
right threat model for an educational game, and a guardrail built around
intent rather than vocabulary preserves the NPC's teaching role. But our own
development numbers overstated how well it worked. On a frozen held-out set,
every backend held only about half of the answer-seeking attacks; failures
concentrated on requests for \emph{part} of an answer, which the detectors did
not recognise and capable models treated as reasonable; and substring scoring
missed most of the leaks that did occur. The practical recommendations are
modest: pair every refusal case with an answer case; keep a frozen held-out
set and report it separately; judge leakage with something that reads
meaning, and audit the judge; and treat a confident wrong answer from a
subject-expert NPC as a failure, not a hold. The next engineering steps follow
directly: recognise partial and disguised answer requests at the input, make
steering persona-neutral, and refuse system-prompt extraction explicitly.
